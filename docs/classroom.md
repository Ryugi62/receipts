# Classroom activity: "Ask the AI for its receipts" (15 minutes)

For a high-school or first-year class that already uses chatbots. Needs one laptop or phone per pair, a browser, and
https://ryugi62.github.io/receipts/ (no accounts, nothing to install). Built on the ML Empowerment curriculum's advice:
"AI can generate incorrect information… Verify with trusted sources."

## Goal
Students leave knowing three things: a chatbot sounds equally sure when it is right and when it is wrong; "no source found" is
not the same as "false"; and the fastest way to check is to find the one sentence that backs a claim.

## Steps
1. **(2 min) Predict.** Show the "Eiffel Tower" sample paragraph. Ask: "Which sentence would you bet is wrong?" Collect guesses.
2. **(4 min) Check.** Pairs press *Check the receipts*. For each claim they copy the label (Backed / May conflict / No receipt)
   into the table below and read the quoted Wikipedia sentence. Answer key: the paragraph says the tower was completed in
   1899; Wikipedia says 1887–1889 — Receipts marks that claim "May conflict" and quotes the sentence.
3. **(4 min) Your own answer.** Each pair asks any chatbot "Tell me about <a person, place or event from today's lesson>",
   pastes the reply, and fills a second table.
4. **(3 min) What now?** Pairs press *Copy a question for the chatbot*, paste it back into the chatbot, and note whether the
   chatbot gives a source they can open, says "not sure", or invents one.
5. **(2 min) Discuss.** Which claims had no receipt? Did any turn out true when you searched yourself? When should you trust a
   confident sentence?

## Teacher notes
- "No receipt" is common (often half of the claims or more on a raw paragraph). That is the point of the lesson: the tool tells
  students where to look, it does not do the checking for them.
- "May conflict" is a hint, not a verdict: on held-out sets between one in four and three in five of these flags fell on a claim humans had marked
  unsupported (docs/eval.md). Ask students to read the quoted sentence before deciding.
- Works best for people, places and events with an English Wikipedia article. Wikipedia itself can be wrong or out of date.
- Privacy: each claim is searched on Wikipedia separately; nothing is sent to an AI company and nothing is stored.
